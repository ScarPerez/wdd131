F1 DICTIONARY

Learn the language of Formula 1.

F1 has a language of its own. Start with some of the terms you will hear most often during a race weekend.

Drag Reduction System

A system that reduces aerodynamic drag on certain parts of the car, allowing it to reach higher speeds on designated sections of the track.

Did Not Finish

Used when a driver starts a race but is unable to complete it because of an incident, mechanical problem, or another reason.

Pole Position

The first position on the starting grid. It is normally awarded to the driver who sets the fastest qualifying time.

Pit Stop

A stop in the pit lane where the team can change tires and perform other necessary work on the car.

Starting Grid

The positions where the cars line up before the start of a Grand Prix.

Safety Car

A vehicle that leads the field at a controlled speed during certain incidents when racing conditions are temporarily unsafe.

RACE WEEKEND

Three stages.
One Grand Prix.

A Formula 1 Grand Prix weekend is more than just the race. Learn what happens before the lights go out.

01 / PRACTICE

Find the rhythm.

Practice sessions give drivers and teams time to learn the circuit, test the car, compare setups, and prepare for qualifying and the race.

02 / QUALIFYING

Fight for position.

Qualifying determines the starting order for the Grand Prix. Drivers push for their fastest possible lap as they compete for the best position on the grid.

03 / RACE

Lights out.

This is the main event. Drivers compete over the Grand Prix distance while managing tires, strategy, fuel, traffic, and changing race conditions.
